# The stranger test — a protocol and a scorecard (session 33; ledger row 33's gate)

The gate for content completeness is a person who has never seen the game
playing a Calm run to wave 5 unaided and being able to say what NEXT
means. This is how to run that test so it produces a filed round of
feedback rather than an impression. Daniil runs it (as a fresh reader, or
with his friend); the observer writes, never helps.

## Before

- A fresh browser profile or a wiped save (SETTINGS → WIPE DATA), so the
  tutorial and the encounter cards fire as they would for anyone.
- The live build, cache-busted (`?cb=<sha>`), at the stranger's own screen
  size; the HUD scale left at its default.
- One sentence from the observer, and no more: "It is a tower defence.
  Hold the Core. I will not help; say what you are thinking as you go."
- A timer. Thirty minutes at most; the run ends when the Core falls, wave
  5 passes, or the stranger stops.

## What to watch (the ten moments)

Score each 0 (did not happen / needed help), 1 (happened after a false
start), 2 (happened cleanly). Write the stranger's words next to it.

| # | moment | what "2" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The first minute | reads the tutorial's first two cards and finds NEW RUN → Calm without a prompt from the observer |
| 2 | Ground | clicks ground beside the road on the first try; says why (near the Core) |
| 3 | The first tower | builds a Bolt from the strip; hovers a button for its card at least once |
| 4 | The first wave | calls it (button or N) rather than waiting for the observer |
| 5 | NEXT | when asked "what is coming next?" points at the strip's NEXT and reads a kind and a mark ("skitter, fast") |
| 6 | The answer | reads the column's answer line under the composition and acts on it, or says why not |
| 7 | The fork | takes a first upgrade (a fork) before wave 6 without being told; can say what the two options were |
| 8 | The offer | when the relic offer comes, reads the cards and picks one (or skips on purpose) within a minute |
| 9 | The card | when an encounter card pops (a new enemy, the first chest), reads it and dismisses it; can say one fact from it afterwards |
| 10 | Wave 5 | the Core stands at wave 5; the stranger can say what they would do differently |

Also note, without scoring: every time the stranger asks "what does X
mean", every misclick that cost Scrap, every place their eye went that
the game did not intend (the observer's guess), and the first thing they
said when the run ended.

## After

- The scorecard total (0-20) and the ten rows go into
  the tracker as **round 34**, every "0" and "1" as an issue titled
  `[r34.<row>] …` with the stranger's words, indexed by the row number above
  (the `triage-round` skill files them).
- A "2" on rows 5, 6 and 10 is the gate; anything else is the next
  session's list.
- The stranger's screen size and browser go in the round's header; a
  score depends on them.

## Why this shape

A stranger test that is a conversation produces an impression; one that
is a scorecard produces a list. Ten rows is enough to see where the
first minute breaks and few enough to score live. The rows are the
tutorial's own steps and the two things the interface must say by itself
(NEXT and the answer), because those are the claims sessions 31-33 made.
