# The carve's variety — what the road looks like, measured and looked at (2026-09-17, session 36)

`node tools/map-sweep.mjs 120` · `--legacy` · `node tools/map-gallery.mjs <threat> <maps>` ·
the app's 7×5 board · 120 seeds per Threat · `node tools/mapgen-sweep.mjs`
for the invariants · `node tools/build-sweep.mjs --debt 120` for what it did
to difficulty.

M5's gate is *"two runs do not resemble each other"*, and the ledger's row
for this session said how: *lane length and turn preference by Threat (Calm
long and gentle, Grim short and knotted); a walk that prefers unused
directions; the fill's land grouped by family; the map sweep gains a
resemblance column.* Nothing measured any of that, so the first job was an
instrument — and the second was to **look at the maps**, because a claim
about what the eye sees is not settled by a column.

Four things, and only one of them was expected:

- **Resemblance was never the problem.** Two maps of a Threat carry the same
  road shape in the same slot on **14%** of slots — chance. Layouts do not
  repeat. The column the row asked for exists now, and its first reading says
  the sameness is somewhere else.
- **The sameness is of character.** Every map on every Threat turns on 55% of
  its two-port slots, give or take a tenth, and never runs straight for
  three. Calm was *knottier* than Grim (0.58 against 0.52): the Threats had
  no walk of their own at all.
- **Lane length by Threat already existed** (92 / 73 / 58 cells). It falls out
  of the entry roll — more lanes, a smaller fair share each — so that half of
  the row's first item needed nothing, and nothing was built for it.
- **The land was salt and pepper, exactly**: adjacent tiles matched 33% of
  the time against a chance figure of 34%.

## What was built

**The walk has a character** (`carve.ts`, `WalkCharacterSpec`). Three numbers,
and all they do is re-weight which *legal* inward move a wandering walk
prefers — no topology rule reads them:

| | `straight` | `spread` | a map's taste runs | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calm | 6 | 2 | 3 – 12 | always avenues |
| Standard | 1 | 12 | 0.08 – 12 | no taste of its own, the widest roll |
| Grim | 0.2 | 2.5 | 0.08 – 0.5 | always knots |

`straight` is the weight of going on the way the walk came, against a turn's
1. **Each map rolls its own factor** between 1/spread and spread (evenly in
the exponent) and keeps it for every walk — that roll is what makes one
Standard map avenues and the next switchbacks. `fresh` (1 on every Threat)
pulls toward the heading this map has walked least.

**The land has regions** (`mapgen.ts`, `LandSpec { regions: 3, bias: 6 }`).
Three centres, one of each land family, and a tile of its region's family is
six times likelier to be dealt there. **The Core's own region is never rock**:
rock cannot be built on, and the ground by the Core is where every tower has
its gift and where the tutorial sends a first-time player.

**With neither option the stream is spent exactly as before.** The engine's
default is the generator as it was: `map-sweep --legacy` reproduces the
"before" tables above **byte for byte**, and the golden hash did not move.
The app asks for both through `threatKnobs()`, so `GENERATOR_VERSION` is 4.

## What it achieved, item by item — and the one it did not

| the row's item | verdict |
|---|---|
| lane length by Threat | **already true** (92 / 73 / 58 cells); nothing built |
| turn preference by Threat — Calm long and gentle, Grim short and knotted | **done for Calm, visibly**: avenue maps 14% → 47%, maps of a few long lanes 21% → 40%, the longest straight 2.5 → 3.5 slots. **Done for Grim, modestly**: 0.52 → 0.58 — it was a thicket before and is a knottier thicket now |
| a walk that prefers unused directions | **built, and small**: it cannot be seen in any one column. Kept because it costs nothing and removes a systematic sprawl, not because it was shown to matter |
| the fill's land grouped by family | **done**: adjacent tiles match 48–50% of the time against a chance of 34% (it was 33%), the family shares and the ore per map barely move, and the regions read at a glance in the galleries — an ore valley, a rocky pass |
| the map sweep gains a resemblance column | **done** — with the walk's character, the land's clumping, `--legacy`, and the gallery |
| **the gate: two Standard runs do not resemble each other by the road's walk** | **moved, not met.** Standard's avenue maps went 8% → 18% and its spread of character widened (turn-ratio sd 0.11 → 0.14, straight-run sd 0.83 → 1.12). About one Standard map in five now differs in *kind*. Four in five are still thickets |

**Why Standard will not move further by this lever — measured, not
supposed.** Standard's roll was tried at 5, 12 and 30. At 5 the sweep could
not tell it from no roll; at 12 avenue maps doubled; **at 30 nothing more
happened**. The walk's taste only decides a *wandering* walk's inward moves,
and on a 7×5 board filled to 84% by eight or nine lanes each lane has three
or four slots of its own — its first is a branch, its last an exit, and what
is left to wander is one or two steps. **The look of a Standard map is decided
by how many lanes it has, and that is decided by the fill target (D28's 0.9),
not by the walk.** Calm moved because it has fewer lanes to begin with.

## What it did to difficulty

The same 120-seed ladder as docs/lab/balance-debt-2026-09-17.md, before and
after. Knotted roads put more road under each tower; regions put rock where
it used to be scattered.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Calm — the know-nothing player holds wave 5 | 100% | **100%** |
| Calm — dies at (median) | 7 | 7 |
| Standard — the reference: mean death / wins | 25.1 / 94% | **24.6 / 91%** |
| Standard — the reference + six common relics: wins | 100% | 97% |
| Grim — the base world: wins | 5% | 9% |
| Grim — the tree (the Laser line): wins | 61% | 64% |

Every rung of the ladder holds. Standard is about half a wave harder and
three points less often won, and that is the variety itself: read apart
(`tail-probe --no-land`, `--no-walk`), the walk alone is neutral (25.1 / 93%)
and the land alone is neutral (25.0 / 94%); together they widen the spread of
maps, and a wider spread has a longer bad tail. **Before the Core's region
was protected from rock it read 24.4 / 89%**; the rule bought two points
back, and is there on principle rather than for them.

The ore ladder's claims were re-read on the new maps (`ore-sweep 400`): 13.0
veins a map (12.5), a tier-2 vein on 30–35% of maps, a tier-3 on 12–16% — so
"about one in nine" is corrected to **about one in eight** wherever it was
written.

## Invariants

`node tools/mapgen-sweep.mjs` deals the **shipped** map now (it used to drop
everything but the two knobs): four boards × eight loadouts × thirty seeds,
**0 failures in 960**. `verifyMap` runs inside `generateMap` on every map
above; a character that broke a topology rule could not have produced a row.

## The instrument

- `harness/src/lab/walkMetrics.ts` — `walkCharacter()`, `resemblance()`.
- `tools/map-sweep.mjs [seeds] [--legacy] [--no-land] [--straight=c,s,g --spread=x --fresh=y --land-bias=b --land-regions=n]`
  — the overrides are how the shipped numbers were found.
- `tools/map-gallery.mjs <threat> <maps> [--legacy] [--coverage=x]` — for
  looking. The averages said the walk lever "barely moved" Calm; the gallery
  showed avenues five slots long. Both were true, and only one of them is
  what a player meets.
- `tools/tail-probe.mjs … [--no-land] [--no-walk]` — reads the two halves
  apart.
- `walk-character.test.ts` — the properties: no options, the same map to the
  last die and the same stream state after; Calm straighter than Grim on the
  same boards; every map of every Threat verifies; the land clumps without
  moving the ore share; the Core's region is never rock.
