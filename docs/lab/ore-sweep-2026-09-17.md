# Ore sweep — the ladder's numbers (2026-09-17; PRD §26, D30)

`node tools/ore-sweep.mjs 400` · the app's 7×5 board · 400 seeds per Threat
for the map half, 120 Standard runs for the income half.

PRD §26 left three numbers to the lab — **spawn chance, vein size, mining
cycle** — and CONTRIBUTING §6 rule 7 says the target is the dev's to state
too. So the targets first, then the measurement that picks each constant.

## The targets, stated before measuring

| # | target | why this and not another |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | With Rich veins bought, **about one map in three** carries a tier-2 vein | "A much lower chance of spawning" (D30). Ordinary ore is on every map (below); one in three is rare enough that finding one changes what the run is for, common enough that a player who bought the node sees it within an evening |
| T2 | With Mother lodes bought, **about one map in eight** carries a tier-3 vein *(written as "nine" at first, off the dealt 8-12%; the census says 13% and the new maps deal 12-16%)* | The same step again: each rung is roughly a third as common as the one below |
| T3 | A tier-2 vein holds **half** a tier-1 vein's Ore; a tier-3 vein **a third** | "A smaller vein" (D30). The vein must still outlast one Refinery for most of a run, or the rare find is a disappointment: a run mines 25–50 Ore |
| T4 | **The map alone clears the tier-2 tree (135 Ore) in about as many runs as tier 1 took (≈19); investing — a vein tile loaded — cuts that to about five** | §26: "a slow, self-funding loop rather than a wall". Luck must be the slow road and the authoring sink the fast one, or nobody spends the ore they find |
| T5 | **Opening a tier moves nothing but veins**: a seed's board, rock and boons are identical with and without the upgrade | A run code names a map. The upgrade should change what a vein is made of, not which map the code means |

## 1. The census — how many veins the dice deal

| threat | veins dealt per map: mean (min–max) | maps with no vein | rare vein at p=0.011 | p=0.02 | p=0.03 | p=0.06 | p=0.1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calm | 12.48 (4–21) | 0.0% | 12.8% | 22.1% | 31.3% | 53.0% | 71.7% |
| Standard | 12.52 (2–23) | 0.0% | 12.9% | 22.2% | 31.4% | 52.9% | 71.6% |
| Grim | 12.45 (3–22) | 0.0% | 12.8% | 22.1% | 31.2% | 52.8% | 71.6% |

- **A map deals about twelve and a half veins**, the same at every Threat.
  The first constant was reasoned from an imagined six and would have put a
  tier-2 vein on more than half of all maps.
- **T1 → p = 0.03 per vein** (31%). **T2 → p = 0.011** (13% analytically,
  8–12% as dealt below, because tier 3 is rolled first and the sample of
  rare events is small).
- **No map in 1,200 is ore-less.** The generator's own comment says a 7×5
  board "rolls ore-less about one time in ten"; that predates the filled
  board (D28) and is now false. Comment debt, filed.

## 2. The shipped constants, as the dice deal them

Spawn `ORE_TIER_SPAWN = [1, 0.03, 0.011]`, vein scale `ORE_TIER_VEIN = [1, ½, ⅓]`.

| threat | tier open | maps with a tier-2 vein | maps with a tier-3 vein | tier-2 vein mean Ore | tier-3 vein mean Ore | tier-1 vein mean Ore | maps whose board, rock or boons moved |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calm | 2 | 36.5% | 0.0% | 30.7 | – | 60.0 | 0 / 400 |
| Calm | 3 | 32.8% | 11.3% | 30.0 | 19.6 | 60.1 | 0 / 400 |
| Standard | 2 | 29.8% | 0.0% | 31.2 | – | 60.4 | 0 / 400 |
| Standard | 3 | 32.0% | 8.0% | 30.7 | 19.7 | 60.5 | 0 / 400 |
| Grim | 2 | 29.8% | 0.0% | 30.7 | – | 60.6 | 0 / 400 |
| Grim | 3 | 31.0% | 12.5% | 31.2 | 19.5 | 60.6 | 0 / 400 |

- T1, T2 and T3 hold as dealt.
- **T5 holds: 0 of 2,400 maps moved — and it did not at first.** The tier
  roll was written as "the last dice on the map stream", which sounds
  sufficient and is not: `generateMap` retries a failed attempt *on the same
  stream*, so an attempt that failed verification had already spent the
  extra dice, and the retry carved a different board. **13 of 400 Standard
  seeds** came out as different maps with the upgrade. The roll now happens
  after `verifyMap` passes. The property was true by argument and false by
  count, which is the case for giving a sweep a column for the thing you
  believe.
- Opening tier 3 does not thin tier 2 (32% vs 30%): the roll goes highest
  tier first, and tier 3 takes one vein in ninety.

## 3. The income — what a run banks

The base world (Bolt, Mortar, Frost, Refinery) with **Rich veins bought**,
Standard, economy 100 Scrap, the reference Railbore line, one Refinery on the
richest vein it may stand on (highest tier first — a player chasing the find).

| maps | runs | tier-1 banked, mean | tier-2 banked, mean (max) | death wave, mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| with a tier-2 vein | 33 | 0.0 | 26.6 (41) | 23.7 |
| without | 87 | 51.0 | 0.0 | 24.1 |
| every run | 120 | 36.9 | 7.32 | 24.0 |

- **A run that finds the blue ore and mines it banks about 27 tier-2 Ore —
  and no tier-1 at all.** That is the trade the ladder offers: the same
  Refinery, the same run, 51 ordinary Ore or 27 rare. The mining cycle
  (`ORE_TIER_CYCLE = [1, 1.5, 2]`, shipped in session 29) is **kept**: with
  the half-size vein it prices a tier-2 Ore at roughly two tier-1 Ore of a
  run's attention, and a 30-Ore vein lasts nearly the whole run (max banked
  41 means a second vein was reached in the best case). No reason to move
  it.
- **T4 holds.** From the map alone a player banks **7.3 tier-2 Ore a run**
  on average: the tier-2 tree (135) in **about eighteen runs**, against
  nineteen base runs for the tier-1 tree when it was priced (session 29).
  With `rich_vein` loaded every run is a tier-2 run — about 27 a run,
  **five runs**. Luck is the slow road; investing is the fast one.
  *(Amended the same day by PR 2: once the shop prices by contents (D31)
  `rich_vein` itself costs 46 tier-2 Ore — about six luck runs — so the
  invested road is **about eleven runs**, still well under eighteen. See
  docs/lab/price-sweep-2026-09-17.md §4.)*
- The reference build dies at **24.0** on Standard here (120 seeds). The
  balance target is 22–24 (session 32); this is the upper edge of it, and
  PR 5 of this session re-measures it properly.
- The opportunity cost is total in this reading because the plan places
  **one** Refinery. A player who builds a second keeps most of the tier-1
  income; the sweep's number is the floor of what the find is worth, not
  the ceiling.

## What the Smith charges for the new cell

PRD §26: *"Placing a tier-N vein costs tier-N ore on top of the lower-tier
ore the tile already costs."* `priceTile` now returns a cost **by tier**
(`OreCost`, index tier − 1): the tile's price in the tier below its richest
vein, as before, **plus tier-N Ore per Ore the vein holds**. This PR shipped
that at 0.5; **PR 2 refit it to ⅓** (`TILE_PRICE.veinTierPerOre`) when the
whole function was fitted against the shipped tiles, so that the smallest
tier-2 vein (30 Ore → 10 tier-2 Ore) is under half of one lucky find. The
numbers and their targets live in docs/lab/price-sweep-2026-09-17.md.

## The instrument

- `MapGenOptions.oreTierMax` — what the tree has opened; the worker and the
  lab both pass `resolveUnlocks(...).oreTierMax`. Absent or 1: no dice.
- `tools/ore-sweep.mjs [seeds]` → `packages/harness/src/lab/oreSweep.ts`:
  the census, the dealt constants with the **moved-maps column**, the income.
- `tools/ore-tier-palette.mjs` derives the tier-2 (blue) and tier-3 (violet)
  twins of the ore's palette roles from the tier-1 colours; idempotent, so a
  re-tint of tier 1 re-derives the others.
