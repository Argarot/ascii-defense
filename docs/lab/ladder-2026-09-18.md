**The short version.** Every rung above "knows nothing" is won by a player
who simply keeps building. The base world wins Grim 91–97% of the time; the
whole tree, 99%. **And the strongest line in the game is its cheapest tower,
never upgraded, as many as the board will hold: plain Bolts at the choke win
Calm 100%, Standard 99% and Grim 84%** — with two to four hundred of them
standing. No health curve opens the gap the ladder wants between the base
world and the tree, because nothing bounds how wide a build may go. That is
a design question, and it is put to Daniil at the end of this document;
**no curve was changed.**

## The instrument — three changes, each of which moved the answer

1. **A plan has a tail** (`LabSpec.tail`, issue #348). Once its listed towers
   and choices are bought it goes on buying — one tower at a time, each taken
   through its choices before the next. The old reference was six towers:
   bought out by wave 12, it died on Grim at wave 21 holding **4,198 Scrap**
   (the last row of the Grim table keeps it, for the record: 8% wins).
2. **The choke is where the LANES gather** (`laneTraffic`). `'choke'` used to
   score a spot by road cells in range; on seed 150474 that stood all five
   towers at a busy junction fifteen cells out while a second branch joined
   the road beside the Core — dead at wave 6 on every plan, and it looked
   like an unwinnable map until the towers' coordinates were read. It now
   scores by how many lanes a tower would see, road cells breaking the tie.
3. **The lab can deal the app's own map for a seed** (`LabSpec.map:
   { threat }`). It used to draw the knobs from one fresh stream and carve
   from another: the same maps in distribution, not the map a player gets.
   The seed corpus needs the second; the ladder still reads the first.

Every sweep prints **towers standing** and **Scrap in hand beside what the
last wave paid**. No tailed plan ends a run holding a wave's income (9–86
Scrap against 260–530 paid).

## The fit — can any curve open L5's gap?

L5 wants the base world at or under 20% and the tree at or over 60%: a gap
of forty points. Grim read at other growth rates, and with its win moved
later (`--final`), 80 seeds:

|---|---|---|---|
| ×1.09, won at 25 *(shipped)* | 91 – 97% | 99% | 2–8 |
| ×1.11 | 89 – 92% | 100% | 8–11 |
| ×1.13 | 71 – 86% | 96% | 10–25 |
| ×1.15 | 48 – 56% | 89% | 33–41 |
| ×1.17 | 29 – 38% | 66 – 69% | 31–37 |
| ×1.19 | 12 – 15% | 44 – 48% | 33 |
| ×1.09, won at **30** | 92 – 98% | 100% | 2–8 |
| ×1.09, won at **35** | 81 – 92% | 99% | 7–18 |

- **No growth rate opens forty points.** The tree is worth about
  thirty-three, at every rate steep enough to show it. ×1.17 is the nearest
  miss (base 29–38%, tree 66–69%) and it costs the middle of the run: the
  base world holds wave 10 on 80–86% of seeds there, against 94–98% today.
- **A later win does almost nothing.** A player who keeps buying out-grows
  ×1.09 for a long time; ten more waves take the base world from 97% to 92%.
- **The tree's thirty-three points are its relics, not its towers.** Its best
  build at every rate is *the base line* holding six relics from the epic
  band. The Laser line with the same relics is level with it or below; the
  Missile line is far below (75% → 4% as the rate climbs); the Tesla line
  sits between. **Nothing the arsenal branch sells out-scales a Railbore.**
- And the fit cannot be finished while Bolt spam stands: at ×1.09 it wins
  Grim 84%, so any rate that holds the *base line* to 20% is being fitted
  to the wrong build.

## What it says

1. **Width is unbounded, and width wins.** A Bolt costs 20 Scrap forever, a
   wave pays 400, and the board has 875 cells. Two hundred and eighty plain
   Bolts win Standard 99%. Upgrading them instead (the forks-only player)
   wins 85% — **buying depth is a worse use of Scrap than buying another
   tower**, on every Threat. The arithmetic is on the roster: a Railbore is
   220 Scrap all told for 30 damage a shot (3.75 times a Bolt's 8), a longer
   reach and a shot that pierces up to three bodies; eleven plain Bolts cost
   the same 220, do eleven times a Bolt's damage, and cover more road.
2. **This corrects docs/lab/economy-research-2026-09-17.md, item E4**, which
   recorded "a rising price per copy" as *not recommended* because "depth
   before width already emerges". It was read off a plan that bought its
   reference line first and went wide afterwards; a plan that goes wide from
   the first Scrap was never played. It is now, and E4 is withdrawn there.
3. **For a build that keeps buying, the early game is the whole game.** The
   reference loses Standard on 2% of seeds and every one of them is lost by
   wave 10; on Grim 9%, every one by wave 15 (the "holds" columns stop
   moving there). A run that survives that far snowballs:
   the reference ends Standard with 41–50 of 50 Core. Machinations' positive
   loop — kills pay for towers that make kills — has no drain once the body
   count caps at sixty.
4. **The "unwinnable" seeds were never the map.** Five of 1,500, all dying at
   waves 7–9 with the mixed line, all won by plain Bolts. On these maps two
   streams of lanes meet only at the Core's door, the first five towers split
   between them, an armoured pack arrives at wave 6 and the income starves.
   A hard opening, not a broken seed. Issue #340 (seed 316773, on the old
   deal) was very probably the same thing seen through the old placement.
5. **L1's two seeds are real** and are the corpus document's business.

## The call — what bounds a build? (issue #357)

This is not a number the lab can derive (CONTRIBUTING §6 rule 7 does not
reach it): it is what kind of game this is. Three answers the genre uses,
each measured against what it would fix here:

| | bounds width | makes upgrades matter | gives the tree a job at the top | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A tower cap, raised by the tree** — the Core supports N towers; the capacity branch sells more | yes, hard | yes — past the cap, depth is the only way up | **yes**: the tree sells the thing Grim needs, which is what L5 asks for | a new rule to teach; N and its steps are the lab's to derive |
| **A price that rises per copy** of a tower kind | yes, soft | partly — spreads a build across kinds | no | every price on the strip becomes a moving number |
| **Leave it** | no | no | no | the ladder stays as it reads above, and Grim cannot be fitted |

**Default if unanswered: the tower cap.** It is the only one of the three
that answers all three findings at once — the spam, the upgrades that lose to
it, and a tree whose top rung sells nothing the base world lacks. The cap's
size, its steps and Grim's re-fit behind it are then one session's work, and
the lab derives every number in it.

It mints itself at the start of the next session, like any call; but it is a
change to what the game is, so the session that builds it opens with a plan
and waits for "go".

## Not done, said plainly

- **No curve moved.** Grim is as it shipped. #349 stays open behind the call.
- **Standard and Calm were not re-fitted** either: their lower rungs fail for
  the same reason Grim's does, and fitting them to a spammer is fitting them
  to the wrong game.
- **The human offset is unknown** (WBS 3.3 needs Daniil's replays). The lab's
  player buys the instant it can afford and places by arithmetic; a person
  does neither. Every win rate here is a ceiling on a person's.
- The tree rows hold six relics from wave 1 (acquisition is not what is
  measured), so the tree's thirty-three points are a ceiling too.
