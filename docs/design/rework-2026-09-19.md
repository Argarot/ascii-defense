# The design review of 2026-09-19, and the rework it produced

**What this is.** The record behind PRD §32. On 2026-09-19 Daniil asked the dev
to stop being a developer for a session: *"assume the role of an experienced
game designer … poke holes … point where the game is flawed by design, not by
numbers."* This file holds the review, the research it stood on (every source
was opened that day; nothing here is from memory), the three rounds of
conversation that turned it into a plan, and what was considered and dropped.
**The spec is PRD §32; the order of work is ROADMAP. This file is the why.**

Nothing in it is built. The build it describes is the one on `main` that day
(golden hash 3521805202, the ladder of docs/lab/ladder-2026-09-19.md).

---

## 1. The review: two decisions with one answer each

A tower defense lives on *where do I build* and *what do I build*. In the game
as it stood, each had one right answer that a player learns once.

**Where.** Every lane merges before the Core (PRD §4.5: "every lane shares its
last stretch"); about four cells in five were buildable ground; a tower takes
one cell and reaches six. A tower on the shared stretch sees all the traffic, a
tower on a lane sees its share, so the shared stretch always wins. The lab
already knew: every winning plan in `plans.ts` places "at the choke", and the
ladder's Calm rows read *plain Bolts by the entry 26%, the same Bolts at the
choke 100%* - the rung is named "placement learned". Lanes are length-balanced
(§4.3.1) and health scales with road length (§9's `L`), which flattens what is
left. Pillar 1 ("the map is the difficulty dial"), the tile library and the
Tile Smith all fed a board whose shape did not change what a player does.

The same absence explains D37. Four hundred plain Bolts won Standard because
ground was unlimited; the answer was plating ("hit big"), which by inference
makes the rapid-fire forks late-game traps (Gatling, Hailstorm, Twin Coil,
Conductor, Ice Shards, Cluster, Salvo, Barrage - eight). A missing map
constraint was patched with a damage-model rule. *The inference about the
forks is not a lab result; PRD §32.12 makes it one.*

**What.** Waves come from one distribution every run, forks are permanent, and
only the next wave is shown. For a newcomer Piercing-or-Shatter is a coin toss;
for a veteran it is solved. Sid Meier's test: an option you always take, or
could take at random, is not a decision.

**The rest, shorter.**

- *Nothing to do in a wave.* Six grunts pay 24 Scrap, a Bolt costs 60: about
  one purchase per 55-second wave early on, and a run may hold no active at all.
- *The counter system is arithmetic.* Five layers of mitigation (type, armour
  or insulation, plating, the floor, bulwark/frontshield). Nobody computes it;
  they obey the panel's answer line - a check, not a choice.
- *The dull relics come first.* By the dev's count about 15 of 49 offerable
  relics break a rule, and several of those are rare or epic, which a new
  player has not won. Hot Loads is literally §7.1's "+15% damage is never a
  story". The lab's own band sweep: the epic band "adds almost nothing on
  average".
- *Ore's spend-or-bank.* Ore spent in a run is taken from permanent progress,
  so the prediction is hoarding until the tree is done and indifference after;
  a Refinery weakens this run to strengthen later ones, which pays for farming
  Calm. *A prediction from how players treat permanent rewards, not telemetry.*
- *The meta sells power, and the signature feature comes last.* "The tree owns
  the pool, never the power" - and the lab says the full tree plays to wave 26
  where the base plays to 13. The Tile Smith opens only when every shop tile is
  owned.
- *Permanence taxes learning.* Permanent forks and a 70% refund punish trying
  things.

**What was right, and stays:** the early call and the next-wave preview; the
legibility discipline (every effect shown and explained); thirteen-minute runs;
relics won by wins; determinism, which makes a daily seed almost free.

## 2. His answers (round one)

1. Scarce ground: **yes, and very scarce** - it was part of why the cell grew.
2. Planner's game, **but sitting back must not be optimal**. Void chests and
   actives were the attempt; not enough.
3. Grim is a **skill check; gear makes it somewhat easier; no-skill play with
   every unlock should almost never win it.**
4. Splitting Ore: conceptually yes, but no third currency - find something
   more elegant. *(Found: bank what you mined. §32.9.)*
5. The player: a **TD player dabbling in roguelites**, with roguelite players
   welcome; casual, browser.

## 3. The research

Two passes, by research agents instructed to open every page. Paraphrased; the
links are what was read. "Snippet only" marks what was seen in search results
and not on the page.

### 3.1 Principles

| principle | source |
|---|---|
| A decision you always make the same way, or could make at random, is not one; good ones are trade-offs, situational, persistent | [Sid Meier, GDC 2012](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-sid-meier-on-how-to-see-games-as-sets-of-interesting-decisions) |
| Judge mechanics by the dynamics they produce; rich-get-richer loops kill tension | [MDA, Hunicke/LeBlanc/Zubek](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf) |
| Let the player choose difficulty inside play, not in a menu | [Jenova Chen, Flow in Games](https://www.jenovachen.com/flowingames/p31-chen.pdf) |
| Competence and autonomy predict enjoyment and return play; clear feedback is a competence requirement | [PENS, Ryan/Rigby/Przybylski](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/player-experience-of-needs-satisfaction-pens/) |
| Fun is learning a pattern; a mastered pattern is boring | [Koster, ten years on](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/raph-koster-s-theory-of-fun-ten-years-on) |
| Units should differ in role, not degree; many X should never equal one Y | [Harvey Smith, orthogonal unit differentiation](https://www.ludism.org/gamedesign/OrthogonalElements) |

### 3.2 Tower defense

- The genre's problem is "towers are doing all the work"; remedies are
  always-available abilities, previews, rewarded early calls
  ([Stardock](https://www.stardock.com/games/article/495008/siege-of-centauri-dev-journal-what-makes-a-good-tower-defense-game));
  total information, one screen, few towers each best at something, and
  lock-and-key enemies as "tax tests"
  ([Doucet, Defender's Quest](https://www.fortressofdoors.com/optimizing-tower-defense-for-focus-and-thinking-defenders-quest/)).
  **Disagreement:** [Casteel](https://waywardstrategy.com/2021/07/27/hard-counters/)
  defends hard counters where scouting exists - and the next-wave preview is
  scouting.
- Fixed build sites concentrate agency, block exploits and let the level force
  risky positions; the cost is strategy players who expect free placement
  ([Thronefall, Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/mastering-minimalism-and-layering-complexity-with-strategy-game-thronefall);
  [interview](https://int-magazine.com/interview/paul-schnepf-of-thronefall/)).
- Failure modes reviewers name in roguelite TDs: the same layout every run
  ([Core Defense reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1203360/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated)),
  cheap 1x1 tower spam and "solved once learned"
  ([Emberward review](https://whatsinkedinmymind.substack.com/p/emberward-review)),
  unlock pool bloat and a stat meta that breaks the ladder
  ([Rogue Tower reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1843760/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated);
  [Dungeon Warfare 2's developer](https://steamcommunity.com/app/698540/discussions/0/1733207382033259459/)).
- **No tower defense with a location-based gold bonus was found**, and no
  treasure-carrier enemy in one. Both ideas below are new to the genre as far
  as this search reached, which is why they are prototyped before they are
  tuned.

### 3.3 Roguelites

- Pick rate and win rate per card police "obvious" and "dead" picks
  ([Slay the Spire](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-slay-the-spire-i-s-devs-use-data-to-balance-their-roguelike-deck-builder)).
  The lab can produce win rate per relic today; pick rate needs players.
- Rewards should steer the run ([Balatro](https://rogueliker.com/balatro-interview/))
  and be felt at once, different in kind ([Dome Keeper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-dome-keeper-focuses-on-systems-that-feed-into-one-another)).
- "Pick 1 of 3" is contested: tightly coupled options make the pick obvious,
  loose ones arbitrary; the fix is a cost on the best option
  ([Burgun](https://keithburgun.net/pick-1-of-3-is-a-missed-game-design-opportunity/)).
  Hence: skipping pays.
- Meta power is a live dispute: an "olive branch" for struggling players
  ([LaGrasta](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/on-roguelikes-and-metagames))
  against a die-learn-retry loop it weakens.
- Losing must pay something out; without a story, that is information
  ([Hades](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-weaves-narrative-rewards-into-i-hades-i-cycle-of-perpetual-death)).

### 3.4 The mechanics of the second round

- **Digging.** The wave timer is what makes digging a decision; information is
  bought (a probe costs a gadget slot); the criticism is a solved dig pattern,
  the same excavation every run
  ([designer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-dome-keeper-focuses-on-systems-that-feed-into-one-another),
  [gadgets](https://domekeeper.wiki.gg/wiki/Gadgets),
  [review](https://gideonsgaming.com/dome-keeper-review/)). **Dome Keeper's
  default visibility rule could not be verified**; §32.2 states our own.
- **Loot carriers** work when harmless, telegraphed, timed and optional
  ([Chance Kin](https://enterthegungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Chance_Kin)); most crowd
  control fails on Diablo's goblin
  ([Blizzard Watch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2021/02/15/treasure-goblin-diablo-3/),
  a fan site).
- **Active play** works as rare, high-value, short-window events (golden
  cookies: every 5-15 minutes, 13 seconds on screen -
  [wiki](https://cookieclicker.wiki.gg/wiki/Golden_Cookie)) and as powerful
  interaction that "needs no rapid tapping"
  ([Pecorella, GDC Europe 2016](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdceurope2016/presentations/Pecorella_Anthony_Quest%20for%20Progress.pdf)).
- **Dismantle economies.** Hearthstone returns 12.5-25% on disenchant and
  crafts deterministically so luck never blocks a player
  ([Blizzard](https://hearthstone.blizzard.com/en-us/news/10245930)); showing
  the whole unlock road made Marvel Snap feel grindy, random rewards "keep hope
  alive" ([Brode](https://kotaku.com/marvel-snap-ben-brode-galactus-shop-deck-interview-ccg-1849978973)).
  Sinks exist to fight inflation
  ([Czerkawski](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/book-excerpt-game-economy-design-metagame-monetization-and-live-operations)).
- **Rewards by difficulty** work as once-only milestones
  ([Hades bounties](https://gamerant.com/hades-how-to-collect-bounties/)) and
  fail when the top tier pays only a leaderboard place
  ([Greater Rifts](https://maxroll.gg/d3/resources/greater-rift-explained)).
- **Endless modes** must be guaranteed to end (accelerating curves:
  [Bloons freeplay](https://www.bloonswiki.com/Freeplay),
  [Brotato](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Endless_Mode)) and must not
  starve income until the build freezes (Brotato's players); one developer
  found his too easy and reset the records
  ([Core Defense](https://ehmprah.itch.io/core-defense/devlog/171713/endless-finetuning-181)).
- **Interest** widened the expert-novice gap in Defense Grid and was removed
  in its sequel, to veterans' anger (player accounts only). Nothing was found
  on why sell refunds sit at 50-80%.

### 3.5 Coming back tomorrow, and money

- Dailies: Spelunky's one attempt made a solo game social
  ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-understated-genius-of-the-i-spelunky-i-daily-challenge));
  Dead Cells scores the first run and lets you practise, because a pure
  one-shot is "unfair" to anyone who watched the seed and unwelcoming to
  everyone else ([Benard and others](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-24-hour-ticket-examining-daily-runs-)).
  Wordle: one a day, no sign-up, no notifications, and no link in the share
  text ([Slate](https://slate.com/culture/2022/01/wordle-game-creator-wardle-twitter-scores-strategy-stats.html),
  [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/12/josh-wardle-interview-wordle/)).
- A daily reward alone "isn't much of a dark pattern"; escalating streaks that
  break on a missed day are, and paying to restore one is worse
  ([darkpattern.games](https://www.darkpattern.games/pattern/11/daily-rewards.html)).
  Duolingo's own test: lowering the bar to keep a streak raised retention
  ([blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)).
- **Stone Story RPG**, as its store pages read that day: premium on
  [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/603390/Stone_Story_RPG/) with no
  in-app purchases; free with ads and purchases on
  [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.MartianRexInc.StoneStory&hl=en_US)
  and the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stone-story-rpg/id1076120218).
  The developer: designed as premium, "then adapted to f2p for mobile"
  ([forum](https://steamcommunity.com/app/603390/discussions/0/3807281795250189742/)).
  A reviewer called the mobile salesmanship aggressive
  ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2023/08/15/stone-story-rpg-review-free-to-play-mobile-download-iphone-android-ipad/));
  store reviews call it mild. Prices were seen in GBP only.
- Portals, from their own docs: Poki bans in-game purchases and removes
  outgoing links, and its exclusive deal runs five years
  ([requirements](https://developers.poki.com/guide/requirements-quality),
  [deals](https://developers.poki.com/guide/revenue-deal-types)); CrazyGames is
  non-exclusive with invite-only purchases
  ([docs](https://docs.crazygames.com/sdk/in-game-purchases/)); itch.io allows
  pay-what-you-want with an open revenue share
  ([docs](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments)). Poki and CrazyGames sync a
  localStorage save under 1 MB to the player's account with no backend;
  Steam Auto-Cloud syncs files.
- The one published supporter-pack conversion found: 6.8% of Steam owners
  ([Into the Radius](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-43549)).

**Not found, so not claimed:** Dome Keeper's default sight rule; an Ironhide
statement on fixed build spots; George Fan on why sun is clicked; any designer
rationale for refund percentages; retention figures for daily modes beyond
Dead Cells' "about 7,000 runs a day"; Patreon or Ko-fi numbers for a browser
game.

## 4. How the plan changed across three rounds

| the dev proposed | Daniil | where it landed |
|---|---|---|
| Pads as an overlay on ground, 25-35 a board | very scarce, yes; and *"there's no plain … pads with rocks on them, or bedrock which is functionally equivalent to void"* | every cell that is not road is a pad, rock over a pad, or bedrock (§32.1) |
| Soft rock, hard rock, bedrock; a dig may find a vein, a cache, a boon, or nothing | *"digging now is doing too much … if it can be dug, there is a pad behind it"*; a boon under rock stays possible; hardness not justified | one rock; every dig is a pad; a rock may hide boon ground and nothing else (§32.2) |
| Dig price rises with the road the pad touches | no - *"only minting in tile Smith"* | a flat dig price; road-touch prices the pad in the Smith alone (§32.2, §32.10) |
| Seismograph: two layers, three at rare, all at epic | *"3 layers deep is pretty pointless"* | epic: two layers; legendary: the whole board (§32.2) |
| Bounty rising along the road, x1 at the Core to x3 at the entry | higher; then: simpler - *pay for how fast the wave was cleared* | the clear-time bonus against a par, spawn windows short; the gradient is the fallback (§32.3) |
| The courier: fast, immune to slows, freezes hold it | *"freezing shouldn't affect courier either"* | nothing holds it (§32.4) |
| Overdrive, an always-on Core ability | make it a run-defining relic; clicking must help without being a grind | - |
| "Hands" relics, one guaranteed in every starting offer; never click-rate | cooldown-gated yes; the guaranteed relic no - *"could be a type of core you select?"* | **Core types** chosen at run setup carry the verb; hands relics modify it (§32.6) |
| Three Cores: Relay, Survey (a lane takes +30%), Siege (a strike) | concept yes, roster no: the lane buff always beats one tower's burst, and a striking Core *is* a tower | roster re-proposed as offence / defence / economy; open as a call with that default (§32.6) |
| One Ore tier | **no** - the ore ladder (D30) stands | tiers stay; they line up with parts (§32.9-10) |
| Tiles after every win | *every run, lost ones too, fewer waves = a lower tier* | every run that cleared wave 5 pays a tile by depth (§32.10) |
| Mint fee = the plain tile plus a quarter of the feature value | parts have no price; they are required, and the fee is the Smith's | mint = the parts painted + §27's fee, extended (§32.10) |
| Surplus parts: infuse two into the next tier; burn one for a one-run feature | infuse yes; burning *"is pretty pointless"*; and the hundreds of roads and rocks need a use | **the part ladder**: commons climb into pads, pads into boons and veins (§32.10) |
| Grim and Endless bought with Ore | - | opened by wins (§32.11) |
| A paid uber tier and paid chests (D27's old intent) | - | recommended against; §32.14 says why; D27 stays his |

**Considered and dropped by the dev:** the first tower beside the Core choosing
the run's verb (a permanent choice made by an incidental click, and nearly
always a Bolt); a guaranteed hands relic (it muddies what an offer is); a
third, run-only currency (his objection, and "bank what you mined" does the job
with none); a fill-a-tile refund for surplus parts (his own doubt, shared).

## 5. What this does not know

- Whether very scarce pads *feel* right. Nobody has played it. It is the first
  thing built, as knobs, and nothing is tuned until he has played three runs.
- Whether plating can go. The hypothesis is that D37's chassis pricing and
  plating have nothing left to do once the map bounds width. §32.12 makes the
  lab answer it; nothing here assumes the answer.
- Whether the clear-time bonus is dominated by one slow body, or taxes slow
  builds. Both are watched in the prototype; the per-kill gradient is the
  stated fallback.
- ~~The Core roster.~~ Answered the same evening (D55): Relay, Bulwark and
  Foreman, with his change that *Brace* halves breach damage and does not
  ignore it. Whether three roles with no common unit really avoid "one is
  always best" is still only an argument until they are played.
- Every number in §32. They are first passes with a target beside each, so
  the lab has something to be wrong against (CONTRIBUTING §6 rule 7).
